7 practical cybersecurity habits everyone should build

Simple steps to help protect your email, social accounts, devices and personal information from common online threats.

By YSCMT Editorial Team · Published 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Everyday activities such as checking email, using social media, shopping online and connecting to public Wi-Fi involve sharing or accessing information. A few consistent security habits can reduce the chance of losing access to an account or exposing personal data.

1. Use a different, strong password for every account

A password reused across several websites creates a chain risk: if one service is breached, criminals may try the same password on your email, banking and social accounts. Use long, unique passwords or passphrases. A reputable password manager can help you create and store them securely.

2. Turn on multi-factor authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds another verification step beyond a password. Where available, use an authenticator app, passkey or security key. If a service offers several methods, choose the strongest practical option and keep recovery codes somewhere safe.

3. Pause before opening links and attachments

Unexpected messages may imitate banks, delivery companies, employers or people you know. Check the sender and the full website address. Be cautious with urgent requests, unexpected invoices, prize claims and messages asking you to sign in. When in doubt, open the organisation's official app or type its known website address yourself.

4. Keep your phone, computer and apps updated

Install operating-system, browser and application updates from official settings or trusted app stores. Updates often fix security weaknesses. Remove apps you no longer use and avoid installing software from unfamiliar links or unofficial download pages.

5. Secure your email account first

Your email account is often the recovery route for many other services. Protect it with a unique password and MFA, review recovery phone numbers and email addresses, and check for unfamiliar forwarding rules or signed-in devices.

6. Back up important files

Keep another copy of important documents, photos and work files. A backup should not be permanently exposed to the same device or account that holds the originals. Periodically check that you can restore a file from your backup.

7. Be careful on public Wi-Fi and shared devices

Avoid accessing sensitive accounts on devices you do not control. On shared computers, do not save passwords, sign out when finished and close your sessions. Keep device screen locks enabled. For public Wi-Fi, use trusted websites and apps, and avoid ignoring browser security warnings.

A simple weekly check: install pending updates, review account sign-in alerts, confirm MFA is enabled on important accounts and make sure your essential files are backed up.

What to do if you think an account has been compromised

  • Use a trusted device to change the affected password and any reused passwords.
  • Sign out other sessions and review recovery details.
  • Enable MFA and regenerate recovery codes if necessary.
  • Contact the service provider through its official website or app.
  • If financial information may be affected, contact your bank or payment provider promptly.

Cybersecurity is not about being afraid of technology. It is about making safer choices consistently. Start with your email account, use unique passwords and MFA, and keep your devices updated.
